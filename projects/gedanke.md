# GeDANKE

GeDANKE is a system of self-created paper coupons: every person issues their own promises on their talents and resources, with an expiry date, kept in their own ledger. This page describes its rules as handbook version 1.7 states them, how the core maps onto them, and where the two differ.

Deutsch: [gedanke.de.md](gedanke.de.md) · Core: [core.md](../core.md)

## What GeDANKE is

GeDANKE stands for "Gemeinsamer Dezentraler Austausch Neuer bzw. Natürlicher Energien" (shared decentralised exchange of new or natural energies). There is no central office and no issuing body. Every person creates their own coupons (GeDANKE-Coupon, GDC), on paper, usable offline. The system, its templates and handbooks are public domain; anyone may use it under its rules.

A GDC is a claim on its creator. Whoever holds it can ask the creator for what is written on it. The handbook compares it to an interest-free loan.

## The coupon

| Field | Status |
|---|---|
| Name and at least one way to contact the creator | required |
| Place, date, signature (or stamp) | required |
| Time value, optionally a value in euro or ounces of silver | required, time value recommended |
| Valid until, or valid for a period from the date of issue | optional, strongly recommended |
| Running number for the creator's own overview | optional, strongly recommended |
| Coupling value in euro or silver, for when the time value cannot be delivered | optional, strongly recommended |
| The community the creator belongs to | optional |

What can be offered: skills (craft, teaching, care, repair, programming, healing) and resources (food, materials, tools, rooms, land). Small services that are not worth registering a business for are named explicitly.

## Value

The unit is time. A coupon lists talents, each with an optional factor: with a time value of one hour, "lawn mowing ×1/4" means four hours of mowing, "dental treatment ×2" means half an hour. Without a factor the factor is one. Products get factors too ("1 kg apples ×1/10"). Every person or community sets their own coupling value; there is no fixed rate between time and euro.

The value of a coupon follows demand for the listed talents and the reputation of its creator. If the creator's skill grows, the coupon grows with it: a coupon issued by a journeyman is redeemed in master's hours once he has passed his exam.

There is no limit on how many coupons a person creates, as long as they issue only as much time as they can actually deliver.

## Circulation

A coupon circulates until it is redeemed with its creator, physically destroyed, or expired. Holders may swap coupons among themselves so that each ends up with talents they can use. Whether the remaining validity is long enough is decided by whoever accepts the coupon.

After the expiry date the creator decides: extend it, accept it anyway, or declare it void. Expiry keeps the ledger short and prevents hoarding. Coupons are not meant as a store of value.

## Default

A coupon fails for two reasons: the creator can no longer deliver (accident, illness, death), or will not (moral objection to the task, or breaking their word). The handbook assumes a default rate of about 5 %.

If the creator cannot deliver, the coupling value is paid out, at the value on the date of issue. This is the only case in which euros are paid. Breaking one's word has no remedy other than avoiding that person and advising others against them; whoever breaks their word repeatedly loses the trust of others and drops out of the communities. Before accepting a coupon of high value, the holder checks validity and whether the creator can be reached. For the case of death the handbook recommends disposing in one's will that all outstanding coupons be paid out.

## The ledger

Every creator keeps a small ledger of their own coupons: number, date of issue, expiry, time value, euro or metal value, and the running total of what is in circulation. Redeemed and expired coupons are struck out. The total shows how much reserve of time and money the creator needs to keep their word. Once all coupons in the ledger have expired, the ledger can be discarded, even if some were never redeemed. The handbook recommends keeping one's own ledger even when a pool offers to keep it.

## Appreciating and buying

Buying is a commercial act between two parties with legal and tax consequences. Appreciating happens only between people within a circle of friends. GeDANKE calls itself a system of appreciation.

## Communities

A GeDANKE community is a group of people who use GDCs among themselves, at least in part, and have their own administration for cooperating with projects outside. Naming the community on a coupon makes it known and strengthens others' trust in the creator. With communities that trust a person and are trusted from outside, larger values become possible (a car, a house), because whoever accepts can fall back on the talents of the whole community, or on a compensation in euro from the whole community.

## Pools

The handbook introduces pools for a transition period, until people trust each other enough that everyone can issue coupons directly to everyone.

**Pool within a community.** A community agrees with a farmer that he accepts its members' coupons for vegetables. The farmer keeps the few coupons whose talents he can use. He does not chase each creator for the rest; he hands the stack to the community's pool and receives the agreed compensation, for example from a pool fund in euro, silver or hours of help. The pool then redeems the coupons with its members or brings them back into circulation inside the community.

**Pools across communities.** Pools to which individuals and several communities connect. Each pool sets its own conditions; people and communities choose a pool and may switch at any time. Pools may cooperate and make agreements among themselves. Anyone may open a pool.

A pool can also show the offers and needs of a community, to its members or publicly; inside a community the community decides, an external pool decides itself. A pool can verify coupons, run a rating system of its choosing, and offer its participants a default guarantee.

Joining a community or a pool is never required.

## Mapping

| Core | GeDANKE |
|---|---|
| Liquid promise | the GDC: issued by a person, held by whoever holds the paper, passed on, redeemed with the creator, expires |
| Content of a promise (what, by when) | talents with factors, time value, validity; coupling value as fallback |
| Ledger of the giver | the ledger, almost field for field |
| Purpose-bound promise | not present |
| Confirmation | not present as an act. Reputation travels by word of mouth; the contact data on the coupon let a holder ask the creator. Pools may run ratings. |
| Vouch | not present as a personal act. The community on the coupon and the internal pool take its place. |
| Trust | "your reputation is your capital"; breaking one's word leads to being avoided |
| Circle | the GeDANKE community: people who use GDCs among themselves |
| Expiry | the validity field, extension at the creator's discretion |

The liquid promise and the giver's ledger are the same thing in both. GeDANKE adds the unit (time with factors per talent) and the coupling value as an agreed fallback, both of which fit into "what" without changing the core.

## Where GeDANKE differs from the core

**The community holds claims and stands for its members.** The internal pool takes the farmer's stack of coupons, compensates him from a pool fund and redeems the coupons with its members. Larger values become possible because the whole community stands behind them. In the core a circle never holds claims, not even a kitty of promises, and never vouches. Read in the core, the internal pool is a set of people who each vouch for members: they take back the coupons, hold them as persons and stand for them, which is what a vouch is (take-back, see [rltp.md](rltp.md)). *To decide:* whether this reading is the core's answer to the pool, and whether the page may state it as such.

**Trust is attributed to the community.** "The larger, more honest and more stable the community, the more trust it has from outside." In the core, trust is only ever in people; a circle makes promises visible but is not trusted. *To decide:* whether "the community is trusted" stays a description of GeDANKE or is translated into "the people who vouch are trusted".

**Projects issue coupons in crowdfunding.** A project issues GDCs for euros, material or work and later pays out in money or produce, as agreed on each coupon. If the project is a circle, this contradicts "a circle never gives promises". In the core it is either a liquid promise by the people behind the project, or a purpose-bound promise of the investors to the project. *To decide:* which of the two.

**Pools as an institution of their own.** External pools exchange, verify, rate and guarantee, as a free market of pools. The core leaves pools out. *Open:* whether pools at the edge of a circle are still needed once vouching exists; the same question stands for cosmolocal.credit and the pools planned in Human Money Core.

**Appreciating and buying.** GeDANKE separates them legally: appreciating only within a circle of friends, buying as a commercial act with tax consequences. The core does not carry this distinction. *Open:* it belongs to the legal edges (legal form, taxes), not to the core.

**No confirmation.** Kept promises leave no record; reputation is spoken. *Open:* nothing to decide, the core adds the confirmation as the step GeDANKE lacks.

**Bearer paper instead of a holder chain.** Whoever holds the paper is the holder; there is no record of previous holders. Forgery is countered by an elaborate signature, a hologram sticker or a QR code, and by asking the creator. The proposal in the seam [promise](../seams/promise.md) gives the liquid promise a chain of holders. *Open:* nothing to decide here; it belongs to that seam.

**Extension at discretion.** After expiry the creator may still honour or extend a coupon. In the core a promise expires; honouring it later is a new promise. *Open:* whether that difference matters in practice.
